# Code of conduct

## The short version

Be decent to people. Assume the person you are replying to is trying to get something right rather
than trying to waste your time. Argue with the work and not with the person doing it.

## What this covers

Everything that happens in this repository: issues, pull requests, code review, commit messages and
the documents themselves. It also covers behaviour elsewhere that is aimed at someone because of
their part in this project.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment of any kind, including unwanted contact after being asked to stop.
- Insults, demeaning comments, or hostility aimed at a person rather than at their work. This
  includes hostility aimed at someone for what they do not yet know.
- Sexualised language or imagery, and unwelcome attention of that kind.
- Attacks or exclusion based on who someone is: their background, their disability, their identity,
  their appearance, their beliefs, their age, or anything else of that shape.
- Publishing someone's private information, or anything that could identify them, without their
  clear permission.
- Deliberately derailing a discussion, or repeatedly reopening a decision that has been made and
  explained, in a way that wears people down rather than persuades them.

Two things that are explicitly fine, because they can look like the list above and are not: saying
that a change is wrong and why, in direct terms; and declining a request with a reason. This
project declines things often, and a clear no is more respectful than an ambiguous maybe.

## Reporting a concern

Open an issue on this repository. That is public, so put in it only what you are willing to have
read by anyone who can see the repository.

**If your concern is about the maintainer**, or you are not willing to raise it publicly, use
[GitHub's own abuse reporting form](https://github.com/contact/report-abuse) instead. It goes to
GitHub rather than to anyone here.

That second route exists because this repository has no private channel to the maintainer today,
and saying so is more useful than pretending otherwise. There is no published contact address, and
GitHub's private reporting features are unavailable while the repository is private. If that
changes, this section changes with it.

## What happens then

There is one maintainer, so enforcement is one person's judgement, applied in whatever time a
project worked on in bursts allows. What that person will do is read the whole thing before
responding, say what they have decided and why, and apply the least severe response that actually
addresses the behaviour: a private word, a public correction, editing or removing a comment,
closing a thread, or blocking an account. Repeated or serious behaviour goes straight to the last
of those.

A maintainer who is themselves the subject of a concern cannot judge it, which is the entire reason
the second reporting route above exists.

## Where this came from
